LexEuropa

An open-model research assistant for EU law. You ask in plain language; it answers in numbered statements, each tied to the exact article or judgment paragraph it comes from, and an independent check labels whether the cited text really supports it. A research aid, not a lawyer.

How it works

Retrieval, writing and checking as separate steps

01

Find the sources

An exact-reference resolver (“Art. 17(1) GDPR”, “C-131/12”), keyword search and meaning-based search are fused into one ranking. Naming a law restricts the search to it.

02

Add the context

The top articles are expanded to their other paragraphs and the rulings of leading judgments are added, because a complete answer needs the whole provision.

03

Write the statements

A small open model, LoRA-tuned for citation discipline, drafts short statements with source handles. A larger stock model takes over if it refuses or misses the question.

04

Check every citation

A cited passage that was never retrieved is rejected by a hard check with no model involved. Each remaining citation is judged separately by an independent model.

05

Warn and withhold

Out-of-library questions get a plain refusal, answers that cite repealed law are flagged, and statements that do not answer the question are withheld.

06

Show the evidence

Statements, their labels and the cited passages appear together in a reading-first interface with a sticky sources panel.

The library

EU law, fetched and checked at the source

81 instruments

Treaties and the Charter; data protection and digital law; consumer, contract and private international law; competition; employment and equality; whistleblowing, free movement, crypto-assets, copyright, cyber resilience and asylum.

38 judgments

Every paragraph plus the operative part (the ruling), from Google Spain, Schrems and Planet49 to Kadi, Francovich, Bosman and Google Shopping.

Current text

Everything comes from the EU Publications Office, with every article and paragraph number checked. Acts amended since publication are read from their latest consolidated version.

Measured

Scored against checklists, with the caveats stated

0.87 completeness

Average share of the key points a complete answer must contain, with 71% of answers fully complete (28 hand-written questions; each key phrase was checked against the provision it is based on).

0.87 / 0.88 citations

Cited-source precision and recall on 25 natural-language questions, with the right source in the top results for 96% of them.

4 of 4 declined

Questions outside the library, such as national tax law, US law or predicting a court case, are refused instead of answered.

These sets are small and each was run once. They were measured on the earlier 31-instrument library; the run on the full 81-instrument library is still to come. Completeness means coverage of the checklist, not legal correctness.

Limitations

What it is, and is not

A research aid

It gives no legal advice. Answers can be incomplete or wrong; the cited text and the labels exist so you can check them.

Not covered

National law, Strasbourg human-rights case law (the source sites refuse automated access), Advocate General opinions, other languages, and judgments from before about 1991.

Open and reproducible

Code under MIT; the fine-tuned model, its adapter and its training data are public. Training data is built from real provision text only; none is model-written.

Models and data

  • Training data is public on the Hub
  • Scale-to-zero endpoint, nothing heavy locally

Quick start

  • Clone the repo and create a virtual environment
  • pip install -e ".[dev]"
  • Put HF_TOKEN in .env, then python -m lexeuropa.cli ingest
  • python3 -m lexeuropa.cli serve

Project layout

  • lexeuropa/ingest fetching and parsing
  • lexeuropa/retrieval hybrid search and fusion
  • lexeuropa/verification citation checks
  • eval/ question sets and graders
